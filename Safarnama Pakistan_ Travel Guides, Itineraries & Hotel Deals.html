Names worth searching

Serena Hotels run heritage and boutique properties in Gilgit, Hunza, Shigar, Khaplu, Swat, Islamabad and Quetta. Pearl-Continental has hotels in Lahore, Karachi, Peshawar, Rawalpindi, Bhurban and Gwadar. PTDC motels exist at many tourist spots and are usually the cheapest official option, though standards vary. When to go

Pakistan has Himalayan summers, desert winters and a tropical coast, so the best season depends on where you are headed.

Spring

March to May
  • Hunza and Skardu apricot blossom (April)
  • Swat and Galiyat
  • Lahore and Islamabad gardens
  • Kalash Chilam Joshi festival (mid-May)

Summer

June to August
  • Deosai, Fairy Meadows, Naran and Kaghan
  • Neelum Valley and Ratti Gali
  • Shandur polo festival (early July)
  • Avoid the plains and Sindh coast heat

Autumn

September to November
  • Hunza golden foliage (October)
  • Kalash Uchau festival
  • Lahore, Multan, Peshawar heritage trips begin
  • Clear skies for K2 and Nanga Parbat views

Winter

December to February
  • Karachi, Thatta, Mohenjo-daro
  • Cholistan desert and jeep rally
  • Makran coast and Hingol
  • Snow at Murree, Malam Jabba and Ziarat

Practical travel tips

The things first-time visitors ask most. Rules and conditions change, so confirm anything official with your tour operator or the relevant authority.

Roads, transport and timing
  • The Karakoram Highway from Islamabad to Hunza takes two days at a comfortable pace; landslides can close sections, especially in July and August.
  • Flights to Skardu and Gilgit are weather-dependent and often cancelled. Keep a buffer day or have a road backup.
  • Start early. Mountain roads are slower than the distance suggests and night driving is best avoided.
Safety, permits and local rules
  • Check current government travel advisories and local news before you go. Conditions vary by district and can change quickly.
  • Some border and high-altitude areas need permits or a registered operator, particularly for foreign visitors and trekking. Confirm current requirements before booking.
  • Tell your hotel or a contact where you are going when you head into remote valleys.
Altitude and health
  • Places like Deosai, Babusar, Khunjerab and Fairy Meadows are high. Ascend gradually, drink water and rest if you get headaches or nausea.
  • Pack a basic medical kit. Pharmacies are limited above Chilas and Skardu.
  • Choose bottled or boiled water and freshly cooked food.
Money and connectivity
  • Carry enough cash in PKR for the north. ATMs are scarce and can be offline above the main towns.
  • Jazz, Zong and Ufone work in many towns; SCOM is usually the dependable network in Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir. Expect no signal in many valleys.
  • Download offline maps before leaving the cities.
Culture and dress
  • Dress modestly, especially outside big cities. Light, loose clothing that covers shoulders and knees works well; carry a scarf for visiting shrines and mosques.
  • Ask before photographing people, particularly women, and always in Kalash villages where there are specific rules.
  • Remove shoes at mosques, shrines and many homes.
Responsible travel
  • Take your waste with you from lakes, meadows and trekking routes; carry a bag for it.
  • Hire local guides and stay in locally owned guesthouses so income stays in the community.
  • Do not climb on or deface heritage sites such as forts, tombs and Buddhist stupas.
